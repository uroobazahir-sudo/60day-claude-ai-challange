DECISION REPORT

Accept the job offer now, or finish the degree full-time first?

The Real Decision

A full-time frontend developer offer has landed mid-degree, with a 2-week response deadline. The real trade-off is real income and momentum now, versus protecting CGPA and a clean, uninterrupted graduation.

It's hard because it isn't just logistics — it's the fear of disappointing family and the fear of an opportunity like this never coming back.

The Case For Each Option

Accept the Job Now

Strongest case for it
Real income, real production experience, and a live job history that outweighs a completion date on most hiring managers' radar.
Hidden upside
Companies increasingly value proven shipped work over a finished-on-time degree — this could compound faster than another semester of theory.
Biggest weakness
Overload risk: job + part-time coursework + the 60-day build challenge at once is a lot to sustain without burning out or dropping CGPA.
Best if you value: momentum and income now

Finish Degree Full-Time First

Strongest case for it
Protects CGPA and keeps graduation on a clean, uninterrupted timeline with full bandwidth to focus.
Hidden upside
Full-time student status may still qualify for scholarships, competitions, or campus opportunities that disappear the moment you're employed.
Biggest weakness
This specific offer may not wait — losing it means starting the job search over later, possibly in a colder market.
Best if you value: stability and a guaranteed finish

Assumption Buster

Assumptions you may be making
  • That this offer is a strict take-it-or-leave-it, with zero room to negotiate a later start or reduced hours.
  • That part-time enrollment will automatically hurt your CGPA — this hasn't actually been tested.
  • That an opportunity this good won't come again after graduation.
Biases at play
Loss Aversion Sunk Cost Fallacy
What you're definitely ignoring

Whether the company would actually agree to a deferred start date or reduced hours — that conversation hasn't happened yet, and it changes the whole calculation.

Premortem

Accept Job Now — failed after 12 months

  • CGPA dropped from juggling job + part-time coursework
  • Burnout from job + degree + the 60-day challenge simultaneously
  • Job culture didn't match expectations, leaving neither strong degree progress nor a good experience
Early warning sign: Missing challenge days or falling behind coursework by month 2.
Prevention action: Negotiate reduced hours upfront and set a hard 3-month review checkpoint.

Finish Degree First — failed after 12 months

  • The offer window closed and no similar opportunity reappeared
  • Regret watching peers gain a year of real experience and income
  • Degree still took longer than planned due to unrelated life events
Early warning sign: Feeling stagnant with no interviews lined up as graduation nears.
Prevention action: Keep applying and networking actively while finishing — don't go passive just because you're "safe."

7-Day Test Plan

Day 1-2
Research the university's part-time enrollment policy and talk to alumni who worked a full-time job while studying.
Day 3-4
Ask the company directly if they'd allow a delayed start date or reduced hours for a trial month.
Day 5-6
Have an honest conversation with your academic advisor and parents about a realistic part-time load.
Day 7
Decision day: if the company agrees to flexibility AND the advisor confirms CGPA risk is manageable — take the job. Otherwise, finish the degree first.

The Verdict

Finish Degree First

It wins narrowly (46 vs 42) on the dimensions that matter most once the excitement fades: stress, reversibility, and regret risk. It closes the burnout door without closing the growth door.

What could flip it: If the company confirms genuinely flexible, reduced hours in writing — Option A's stress and reversibility scores rise enough to overtake.
The "once in a lifetime offer" feeling is loss aversion talking, not the market — strong roles come again for people who finish strong.
